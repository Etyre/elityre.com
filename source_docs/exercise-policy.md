# Exercise policy

It is a priority for me to exercise at least a little, in some way, almost every day.

When I’m in the habit of exercising every day, its relatively easy to maintain the habit. It’s only booting up from a cold start that’s effortful. So I exercise every day. I probably miss around one day out of 30, during my regular life.

I do make an exception for days when I’m fasting. I don’t generally do heavy exercise if I’m not eating that day.

I typically go exercise as a break during my first work block.

# **Exercise Programming**

I alternate between:

- Barbell squats
  - Doing squats regularly is most important. For some reason doing squats has a huge impact on my well being that no other exercise does.
- Deadlifts [no more than 150 lb total]
- Overhead barbell press
- Bent over barbell rows
- Barbell bench press
- Weighted chinups
- Weighted pullups
- Cardio Interval training

I’m not picky about scheduling. I try to make sure that I do each exercise at least once a week and no more frequently than every three days. Most days I’ll only do one exercise, but I’ll do multiple in a day if I’ve gotten a bit behind, or I just feel like it.

My basic philosophy is to always be doing *slightly* more than last time, along some dimension: more weight, more sets, more reps per set, or less time between sets. I hit this target (guessing) around 19 times out of 20.

# Strength training

### **Barbell training**

For the barbell exercises, I’ll typically do 5 sets of 5.

My goal is to increase the weight slightly, every session, even if only by 1.25 lbs each side, or to increase the number of reps or the number of sets, from the last time.

If I don’t feel up for pushing my maximum, I’ll sometimes do markedly less weight (maybe 60 or 70% of my maximum), but I’ll do many more sets or reps per set.

I use an interval timer to time the sets. (Otherwise I’m apt to get lazy and spend long minutes between sets, and my workout ends up taking an hour for one exercise). For heavy barbell training the intervals are:

- 1 minute work
- 2 minute 40 seconds rest.

Though in practice, I’ll finish the set before the 1 minute is up, so my rest is a bit longer than 2:40.

When a barbell exercise feels really hard, I’ll ask myself “can I do it?”, and if the answer is yes, then I do it. (A pattern I picked up from Duncan Sabien, who I reports it from David Belle).

### **Chinups and pullups**

I always do at least 40 pull-ups and chin-ups, typically in sets of around 10. If I’m doing chin-ups or pull-ups without additional weight, I’ll do more reps per set. My goal is to build up to being able to do 25 consecutive unweighted pull-ups and 25 consecutive unweighted sit-ups.

I do pull ups and chin-ups on an interval schedule of one set every 4, 3, or 2 minutes, depending on how much weight I’ve added.

# **Cardio Interval Training**

In addition to strength, I also do cardio interval training about twice a week - typically rowing, which is low impact.

Using a heartrate monitor and an interval timer, I’ll alternate exerting myself hard, keeping my HR around 170 bpm, and slowing down, keeping my HR around 140 bpm, for a total of 5 cycles.

The intervals are 1 minute of intense exertion, followed by 1 to 2 minutes of reduced exertion.

(The basic idea here was taken from chapter 16 of Josh Waitzkin’s *The Art of Learning*, in which he claims that this kind of training boosts momentary recovery, and therefore cognitive performance.)

# In-life running

Every time I walk somewhere, run for at least part of the way.

I’ve set myself up to make this feasible. I’m almost always wearing sneakers, and I wear a backpack that is comfortable to run with.

# **Other**

The above are my staples, but I’ll sometimes end up up physically exerting myself in other ways.

- If I find myself traveling and don’t have access to a gym, I might find a ~knee-high ledge and jump on it and off it 100 times, or I might run up the stairwell of a multi-story building.
- I sometimes spend hours walking around the neighborhood while listening to and taking notes on some audio content. My rule is that that I’ll count this as my exercise for the day only if I walked enough that my feet are sore, which is typically around 20,000 steps, according to my Oura ring.
- I’ve enjoyed playing Beatsaber and Blade and Sorcery in the past, with weights on my wrists to increase the exertion per move.
