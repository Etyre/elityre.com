# Exercise policy

It is a priority for me to exercise in some way, almost every day.

I optimize my exercise to support the effectiveness of my sleep, to increase my daytime energy and focus, and to stave off long term cognitive decline.

Subjectively, it feels that exercising every other day is sufficient to maintain the cognitive benefits of exercise. However, when I’m in the habit of exercising every day, it is relatively easy to maintain the habit. It's much more of a slog when I'm booting up from a cold start, after a few days of inactivity.  So I exercise every day. I probably miss around one day out of 30, during my regular life. (I do make an exception for days when I’m fasting. I don’t generally do heavy exercise if I’m not eating that day.)

I typically exercise early in the day, as a break during my first work block, or during my organization's lunch time.

# **Exercise Programming**

I alternate between cardio interval training and strength training.

My basic philosophy is to always be doing *slightly* more than last time, along some dimension: more weight, more sets, more reps per set, or less time between sets. I hit this target (guessing) around 19 times out of 20.

# Strength training

I generally rotate between the following exercises:

- Barbell squats
  - Doing squats regularly is most important. For some reason doing squats has a huge impact on my well being that no other exercise does.
- Deadlifts [no more than 150 lb total]
- Overhead barbell press
- Bent over barbell rows
- Barbell bench press
- Weighted chinups
- Weighted pullups

I’m not picky about scheduling. I try to make sure that I do each exercise at least once a week and no more frequently than every three days. Often, I’ll only do one exercise, but I’ll do multiple in a day if I’ve gotten a bit behind, or I just feel like it.

### **Barbell training**

For the barbell exercises, I’ll typically do 5 sets of 5. 

My goal is to increase the weight slightly, every session, even if only by 1.25 lbs each side, or to increase the number of reps or the number of sets, from the last time.

If I don’t feel up for pushing my maximum, I’ll sometimes do markedly less weight (maybe 60 or 70% of my maximum), but I’ll do many more sets or reps per set.

I use an interval timer to time the sets. (Otherwise I’m apt to get lazy and spend long minutes between sets, and my workout ends up taking an hour for one exercise). For heavy barbell training the intervals are:

- 1 minute work
- 2 minute 40 seconds rest.

Though in practice, I’ll finish the set before the 1 minute is up, so my rest is a bit longer than 2:40.

### **Chinups and pullups**

I always do at least 40 pull-ups and chin-ups, typically in sets of around 10. If I’m doing chin-ups or pull-ups without additional weight, I’ll do more reps per set. My goal is to build up to being able to do 25 consecutive unweighted pull-ups and 25 consecutive unweighted sit-ups.

I do pull ups and chin-ups on an interval schedule of one set every 4 or 3, depending on how much weight I’ve added.

# **Cardio Interval Training**

I do cardio interval training approximately every other day. Doing Cardio Interval training has a marked impact on my night time HRV.

Most typically, I'll do a series of short, timed, sprints on a treadmill. (It's much easier to do this on a treadmill, where I don't have to push myself to keep sprinting, I *have* to sprint to keep up with the treadmill, and I just have to not stop.)

Using a heart-rate monitor and an interval timer, I’ll alternate exerting myself hard, keeping my HR around 170 bpm, and slowing down, keeping my HR around 140 bpm, for a total of 4 cycles.

(The basic idea here was taken from chapter 16 of Josh Waitzkin’s *The Art of Learning*, in which he claims that this kind of training boosts momentary recovery, and therefore cognitive performance.)

### Targeting 170 bpm

My routine is optimized so that my heart rate is at 170 bpm or above, for the whole length of each of the high intensity sprint intervals. 

* I set the treadmill speed to 10.7 mph during the high intensity intervals
* I include a 35 second "ramp up" interval immediately before each high intensity interval, to allow the treadmill to get up to speed, and to allow my heart rate to get up to 170 bps.

For me, those parameters work for getting my heart rate up to 170 bps, as fast as possible, without exerting myself more than is necessary to hit that target.

### Routine

Over the course of the session, I alternate between a treadmill speed of 10.7 mph (a moderately intense sprint) and 2.5 mph (a brisk walk). 

*Warm up* 

I start each session with a 2 minute warm up. I set the treadmill to 10.7, and sprint until my heart rate reaches 160 bpm. When I hit 160 bpm, I immediately drop the speed to 2.5 mph, until the end of the two minutes. Quickly raising my heart rate like this, and then letting it fall, means that my heart rate is heightened before the first ramp up interval, to ensure that I get up to 170 by the end of that 35 seconds.

*Cycles*

Then I'll do 3 to 4 cycles of...

1. Ramp up - Set the treadmill to 10.7 mph and keep pace as it speeds up.
2. High intensity interval - Sprint at 10.7 mph
3. Lower intensity interval - Set the treadmill to 2.5 mph and slow down correspondingly

I have gradually increased the lengths of the high intensity intervals and decreased the lengths of the lower intensity interval. 

I started with 30 seconds of HI and 120 seconds of LI. Over the course of a few months, I gradually extended the HI intervals by a second at a time, until they were 45 seconds each. When I have extended them to 60 seconds, I'll start shrinking the LI intervals, shaving off a second at a time, until they're only 60 seconds.

Most days I'll do 4 cycles. But if I'm feeling tired or less capable, I'll do only 3.

# In-life running

Every time I walk somewhere, run for at least part of the way.

I’ve set myself up to make this feasible. I’m almost always wearing sneakers, and I wear a backpack that is comfortable to run with.

# **Other**

The above are my staples, but I’ll sometimes end up up physically exerting myself in other ways.

- If I find myself traveling and don’t have access to a gym, I might find a ~knee-high ledge and jump on it and off it 100 times, or I might run up the stairwell of a multi-story building.
- I sometimes spend hours walking around the neighborhood while listening to and taking notes on some audio content. My rule is that that I’ll count this as my exercise for the day only if I walked enough that my feet are sore, which is typically around 20,000 steps, according to my Oura ring.
- I’ve enjoyed playing Beatsaber and Blade and Sorcery in the past, with weights on my wrists to increase the exertion per move.
